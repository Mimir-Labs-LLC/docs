# Structured Demo Agenda

*Demo-first, proof-oriented. Shows what is built, where humans validate, and what remains roadmap — honestly. Target length ~45–60 minutes. Every step ends by tying back to a business consequence, not a feature.*

---

**Framing line to open with (before any screen):**
> "We're not going to pitch an ERP. We're going to show you how we find where your records stop telling the same truth, and how one slice can be made to run on records you can trust. I'll be clear as we go about what's live, what's facilitated by us, and what's roadmap."

| # | What to show | Why it matters (say this) | ~Time | Live / Facilitated / Roadmap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Diagnostic ingestion on a simple source.** Point the diagnostic at one clean system; show it ingest and structure the environment quickly. | "In minutes, not a project, we can read an environment and start structuring it." | 5 min | *[confirm]* |
| **2** | **Definition / label discovery + the review flow.** Show the tool proposing what terms mean, then the point where a human validates. | "This is where software ends and your people confirm. We surface candidates; your SMEs decide. Nothing is asserted as truth without a human." | 8 min | *[confirm — this is the honesty beat; be explicit about the human-in-the-loop boundary]* |
| **3** | **Diagnostic output — before and after workshop review.** Show raw signals, then the same findings after human review, reframed for IT strategy, audit/close, and governance audiences. | "Raw signals become business guidance. The same finding speaks to a CIO, a controller, and an auditor differently — because they act on it differently." | 10 min | *[confirm]* |
| **4** | **Messy multi-system example.** The real wedge: SAP + Excel + QuickBooks + CRM, where the system, the spreadsheet, the workflow, and the report disagree. | "This is the real problem. A clean SAP box hides it. Your world looks like this — and this is where the reliability risk actually lives." | 10 min | *[likely to-produce — see next-materials; may be a rough/partial run at first]* |
| **5** | **One governed workflow (governed state change + policy enforcement).** Take one path; attempt an action that violates a policy; show it blocked *before* the record changes, with a deterministic reason; show the audit trail generated by the path. | "This is the difference between watching and governing. The rule fires before the write, not after. The evidence is generated, not reconstructed." | 10 min | *[confirm maturity — this is the ROPE beat; scope to what's demonstrable]* |
| **6** | **Proof-cell proposal.** Turn the demo into a concrete next step: one narrow path, bounded scope, weeks, defined success criteria. | "Here's how we prove this on your data, on one slice, in weeks, without touching your ERP." | 7 min | Proposal (see `02-governed-operations-proof-cell.md`) |

## Rules for running it

- **Translate the names once, then use buyer language** (see `01-positioning-and-market-language.md`). Don't say "Ratatosk" and "ROPE" cold.
- **Every step lands on a consequence** — close, audit, migration, integration, production, delivery, or decision confidence — not on a feature.
- **Be first to name the limits.** Say what's live, what you facilitate, and what's roadmap *before* they ask. Volunteering the boundary is what makes the demonstrable parts believable. (BFTP flagged this explicitly.)
- **Steps 4 and 5 are the proof of the thesis.** If you're short on time, protect those two; compress 1–3.
- **End on the proof cell, always.** The demo's job is to earn a scoped next step, not to close a platform sale.

## What to have ready

- The **messy multi-system diagnostic run** (or the best available partial) — the single most important asset to have before this demo; it's the only step that tests the real wedge.
- The **false-positive walkthrough** (Document Number vs. Document Type) for step 3, so you can show you distinguish real business-definition conflict from normal ERP structure.
- The **customer-ready executive diagnostic** (template in `04-*`) as the leave-behind.
