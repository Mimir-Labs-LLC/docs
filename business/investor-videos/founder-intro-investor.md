# Founder Introduction — Investor Version

**Target length:** 3-4 minutes
**Format:** Founder on camera, direct address
**Audience:** Pre-seed investors, accelerator program reviewers
**Setting:** Clean background, business casual. Confident, concise.

---

## SCRIPT

[On camera. Direct, measured. Not pitching — explaining.]

Hi, I'm Doc Gaither. I'm the founder of Mimir Labs.

I spent fifteen years in manufacturing — on shop floors, in planning meetings, running operations. And I spent most of those years fighting with ERP systems that couldn't give me a straight answer about inventory, production status, or what a customer actually ordered.

The problem wasn't the ERP's features. It was the data. After five years of custom fields, workarounds, and consultant patches, nobody trusted it. So every department kept their own spreadsheets. The ERP became a quarter-million-dollar-a-year filing cabinet.

I started writing VBA workarounds to bridge the gaps — macros to reconcile inventory, scripts to validate data between systems, tools to make the ERP do what it should have done out of the box. Eventually I realized I wasn't patching a system. I was building a shadow system on top of a broken one.

Mimir Labs exists to build the real thing.

[Brief pause]

### The Market

The manufacturing ERP market is over $10 billion and growing at 8-10% CAGR. The mid-market — manufacturers with 10 to 500 employees — represents 80% of the customer base but is dominated by aging platforms: Epicor, Sage, Infor, Dynamics. These companies are locked into systems that are expensive to maintain, expensive to customize, and expensive to leave.

That lock-in is our opportunity.

### What We've Built

Six products, one platform.

**Yggdrasil ERP** is the manufacturing platform — ten integrated modules, one shared data model, zero custom fields. The data model doesn't drift because there's nothing to customize.

This constraint unlocks something no other ERP can offer: a real-time B2B Event Hub. A purchase order in one tenant becomes a sales order in the supplier's tenant — with zero translation — because every tenant runs the identical schema. No EDI. No middleware. No mapping tables.

No incumbent can replicate this. SAP, Epicor, NetSuite — they all allow custom fields. That means no two installations have the same schema, which means cross-tenant data exchange always requires a translation layer. They can't remove customization without destroying their installed base. We started without it, which means we're the only platform where the network effect is architecturally possible.

Every manufacturer on the platform makes the platform more valuable to every other manufacturer. Each customer pulls their trading partners onto the system. The switching cost compounds with every connection.

And "zero custom fields" doesn't mean the model is frozen. When a data point proves its value across the ecosystem, it becomes canonical — every tenant gets it. When a customer needs something specific to their operation, it lives in a research sidecar: observable, exportable, but isolated from the canonical schema. The canon evolves through governance, not individual customization.

**Ratatosk** and **Ragnarok** are our go-to-market wedge. Ratatosk is a one-day data governance workshop — $9,500 — that gives manufacturers clarity about their existing data landscape. When that workshop reveals the mess in their legacy system, Ragnarok provides the governed migration path. These services generate revenue today while building the pipeline for ERP subscriptions.

**Bifrost** is live integration between systems — real-time CDC sync with semantic mediation. This is what keeps customers on the platform after migration.

**Jormungandr** ensures the governance baseline doesn't decay over time — continuous drift detection and compliance enforcement.

**Norn** is contract intelligence — a parallel SaaS product with a free tier that generates inbound leads and standalone recurring revenue at $99-$1,499/month.

### The Business Model

Three revenue streams:

**Services** — Ratatosk workshops at $9,500-$14,500. Low-friction entry point. 50% of the fee credits toward migration if they engage within 12 months. This is the door opener.

**Implementation + ERP subscriptions** — $25K implementation, $35K-$250K/year recurring. This is the core revenue engine.

**SaaS** — Norn at $0-$1,499/month. Separate product, separate market, parallel revenue stream with a free tier for inbound.

### Why Now

Three forces are converging. First, the Epicor and Sage installed bases are aging out — their architectures are 15-20 years old and the upgrade paths are painful. Second, data governance is becoming a board-level conversation as manufacturers face supply chain complexity, PE roll-up integration, and audit requirements. Third, the cost of building enterprise software has dropped dramatically with AI-assisted development — a small team can now build what used to require fifty engineers.

### Traction

We're pre-revenue. The platform is in alpha. Norn is piloting with a Business tier at $299/month. We have a validation cohort targeting five manufacturers in South Central Pennsylvania through Penn State LaunchBox and MANTEC partnerships.

I won't tell you we have product-market fit yet. What I will tell you is: the product works, the architecture is sound, and the first paying customers are a Ratatosk workshop away.

### The Ask

We're raising pre-seed capital to fund two things: the first three Ratatosk engagements that become case studies, and one customer-facing hire who can run workshops and implementations while I continue building. The goal is $500K ARR within 18 months — at which point the business model proves itself.

[Direct to camera]

I built this because the companies that make real things deserve software built with the same rigor they bring to their own work. If that thesis resonates, I'd welcome the conversation.

Thank you.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Tone:** Confident, specific, no hand-waving. Investors hear "we're disrupting" 50 times a week. This should sound like an engineer explaining a structural opportunity, not a salesperson hyping a vision.
- **Numbers matter:** Market size, pricing, traction status — be specific. "Pre-revenue" is honest and investors respect it more than inflated claims.
- **The VBA origin story:** This is the credibility moment. It shows Doc isn't a developer who read about manufacturing — he's a manufacturing operator who learned to code because the tools failed him.
- **Don't oversell traction:** "Pre-revenue, alpha, validation cohort" is honest. Don't claim PMF you don't have.
- **Closing frame:** Mimir Labs logo, doc@mimirlabs.net, mimirlabs.net
- **No music.** This is a trust video.
