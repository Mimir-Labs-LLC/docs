# George Call Cheat Sheet

For the upcoming call with George, host of *The ERP Perspective*. One screen. Full script: `business/Objection handling.txt` (George Call Flow Script section).

## Opening line

> Before I get too deep into product, I want to test the premise. In your experience, how often is the ERP actually where the business runs, versus where the business reports through?

## Five discovery questions

1. What is the most common reason guests on your show give for an ERP project disappointing the customer — and how many would you actually believe?
2. When two reports disagree, which one does the operator trust, and why?
3. When somebody tells you the data is dirty, do they distinguish between dirty data and missing operational meaning, or is it all hygiene?
4. In the AI conversations you’re hearing from vendors right now, who is talking about the substrate the agent acts on, and who is just talking about the agent?
5. If you described the system-of-record-versus-system-of-reality gap to people in your network, who would recognize it without needing it explained?

## Five likely objections — short responses

- **“This sounds like data governance.”** → Adjacent, but it’s the wrong center of gravity. Governance describes data after the system created it. We govern when the event is recorded, not after.
- **“This sounds like MDM.”** → MDM answers “is this the same supplier.” The harder question is “was this supplier approved for this part revision when this order shipped.” That’s operational truth, not entity resolution.
- **“This sounds too big.”** → The entry is small. A diagnostic engagement on one value stream. Replacement isn’t the first ask. Proof is.
- **“Is this an ERP replacement?”** → Eventually, for customers who decide they want the substrate. Not for the ones who only need the diagnostic. Both are fine outcomes.
- **“Why now?”** → Because agents take the human workaround layer out of the operating model. The substrate that was good enough for human-mediated systems isn’t good enough for agent-mediated ones.

## 60-second product explanation

The thesis is that most ERP projects fail because the system never fully contained the business. The business runs in spreadsheets, exception meetings, side databases, and the heads of people who know when the documented process doesn’t apply. We start with Ratatosk — a five-to-eight-week diagnostic that follows one value stream or product line through every system and side store the customer runs, and produces a written model of where operational meaning is being lost. That’s the entry. The strategic destination is Yggdrasil ERP, a governance-native ERP that carries the operational model — states, authorities, policies, exceptions — as first-class data, enforced at the moment a transition is recorded. Customers who want the diagnostic get the diagnostic. Customers who decide they want the substrate get a platform built around it. The diagnostic is honest standalone; the platform is the answer for the ones who choose it.

## Best closing ask

> Of the customers in your network who would understand the diagnostic engagement, is there one you’d be willing to introduce me to? I’ll handle the rest of the conversation; the introduction is the only thing I’d ask for.

**Fallback if the pilot intro isn’t the right close:** ask for a written reaction in 24 hours — three sentences. If the frame holds up, push harder. If it doesn’t, learn now.

## Don’t

- Don’t lead with product. Don’t name Yggdrasil or ROPE until the substrate question is on the table.
- Don’t run discovery as a checklist. Two or three deep places beat fifteen shallow ones.
- Don’t over-explain Norse naming.
- Don’t conflate dirty data with incomplete operational meaning. The distinction is the whole point.
- Don’t ask for an investor intro unless he raises it. We’re not raising.
