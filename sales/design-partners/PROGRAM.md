# Mimir Labs Design Partner Program

**Status:** Draft v1 — 2026-05-07. Not yet ratified or executed with any partner.

**Purpose.** A structured early-customer engagement that gives a small or under-served prospect access to Yggdrasil ERP at a forgiving price in exchange for a mandatory ongoing dialogue that produces real-world product feedback. Mimir Labs sells software access at a discount; the design partner sells access to their operational reality at a discount. Both sides give something they have plenty of in exchange for something they don't.

This is **not a charity arrangement**. The exchange is the point. A free customer doesn't engage; a paying customer with a structured feedback obligation does.

---

## Who qualifies

The Design Partner Program exists for prospects who are **too small or too early** for the standard Yggdrasil commercial pricing to make sense, but whose operational reality is exactly the segment Mimir Labs is building for. Concretely:

| Qualifier | Why |
|---|---|
| Manufacturer (CNC, fabrication, light assembly, custom production) | The platform is built around manufacturing operations. Non-manufacturers should not be enrolled in this program; they're better served by a different motion |
| 1–10 employees | Below this size, ERP is overkill in any form. Above it, the customer can afford standard pricing |
| Currently running on QuickBooks + spreadsheets, or similar minimal-tooling setup | Means there's a real upgrade path Yggdrasil can serve |
| Owner-operator or small leadership team | Decision-making is fast, the dialogue is with the actual operator |
| Geographic reachability for at least one in-person visit per year | The dialogue is qualitatively better with at least one shop visit; remote-only partners are still possible but lower-priority |
| Willingness to engage on the structured cadence (see "The dialogue" below) | This is the consideration. A partner who won't engage is a discount, not a partnership |

A prospect who fits all of the above is a candidate. A prospect who fits five of six can still be a candidate; ask before adjusting more.

A prospect who is too large for the program but interested in being a "design customer" is a different shape — handle as a custom enterprise relationship, not under this program.

## The exchange

| Mimir Labs gives | The Partner gives |
|---|---|
| A hosted Yggdrasil ERP instance (all 10 modules enabled) | A monthly 30-minute structured dialogue call |
| Light onboarding (one-hour kickoff, account provisioning, walk-through) | A quarterly half-day shop visit (in-person if geography permits, otherwise extended remote session) |
| Best-effort support: email/Slack response within 1–2 business days | Open feature requests and bug reports as they encounter them |
| Discounted rate locked for 24 months ($49–$99/month, set at deal time) | Permission to use anonymized stories and operational details in Mimir Labs marketing, essays, case studies, and sales material |
| Future cohort program access (E2G, etc.) at no additional cost | Honest answers to operational questions during the dialogue cadence |
| Influence on the product roadmap | Skin in the game — they pay something, even if discounted |
| 90-day mutual review with clean exit if it isn't working | A 90-day review willingness on their side |

## Financial structure

- **Rate:** $49/month or $99/month, set at deal time. Default to $99 unless there's a specific reason to soften (e.g., the partner is pre-revenue or a charitable/educational case).
- **Term:** 24 months, with 90-day mutual review at the start.
- **Payment:** Monthly. ACH preferred, card acceptable. No annual prepay required.
- **Rate lock:** The discounted rate holds for the full 24 months. After the term, the partner moves to standard commercial pricing (currently being defined; assume a reasonable transition).
- **Exit:** Either party can end the agreement at the 90-day mark with no penalty. After 90 days, either party can end with 30 days written notice — the agreement is structured for honest evaluation, not lock-in.

The financial structure is intentionally not a real commercial rate. The point is to make the relationship real (skin in the game) without making the price the gating factor. **Don't haggle on price** — if a prospect can't or won't pay $49/month, this is the wrong fit for them.

## What the partner gets (services in detail)

- **Yggdrasil ERP — hosted instance.** Multi-tenant, all 10 modules (CRM, Sales, Purchasing, Manufacturing, Warehouse, Finance, Projects, PLM, Quality, Service) enabled. Web app + desktop client both available.
- **Onboarding kickoff (1 hour).** Logins, walkthrough of the modules likely to be relevant for the partner's actual work (typically Sales, Manufacturing, Warehouse, Finance for a CNC shop), basic data entry orientation. Single follow-up doc with URLs and credentials.
- **Best-effort support.** No SLA. Email response target 1–2 business days. Bugs they hit get logged in Jira; feature requests get triaged into the roadmap conversation.
- **Future cohort access.** If/when Mimir Labs runs E2G or other federally-funded TA programs, the design partner is invited at no additional cost.

What the partner does **not** get:

- Ratatosk, Ragnarok, Bifrost, Norn beyond the design partner agreement signature itself, or any other Mimir Labs product. The platform offer is Yggdrasil ERP only. Adding more confuses the relationship.
- Custom development. Feature requests are heard and prioritized; they are not commitments.
- An SLA, on-call, or production-grade support guarantees. This is design partnership, not enterprise SaaS.
- A pricing precedent. The discounted rate is specific to this program; future commercial customers do not get it.

## The dialogue (the real consideration)

The dialogue is what Mimir Labs is actually buying. Without it, the program is just a discount.

### Monthly call (30 minutes)

Structured, scoped, on a recurring calendar invite. The first ten minutes are theirs — what they did with the platform that month, what worked, what didn't, what they routed around. The next ten are yours — questions tied to a rotating monthly theme (jobs, quoting, invoicing, inventory, customer communications, etc.). The final ten are loose — feature requests, surprises, anything else.

If the partner skips two consecutive months without rescheduling, the agreement is in breach. The dialogue is mandatory, not aspirational.

### Quarterly shop visit

Half-day on-site if geography permits. Watch the partner work. See the paper, the whiteboard, the spreadsheet, the napkin tracking. Pay attention to what they don't say but do habitually — that's the data point most ERPs never see and most product roadmaps never reflect.

If geography doesn't permit, an extended remote session (90 minutes, with screen-share of how they actually use their tools) substitutes. One in-person visit per year is the minimum even for remote partners; budget for travel.

### Feature requests and bug reports

Every interaction the partner has with the platform that surprises them — good or bad — is a data point. The dialogue cadence makes it easy for them to surface these without feeling like they're pestering. Each request gets logged in Jira (under a `design-partner` label or epic), and the partner gets visibility into status.

The fact that *some* of their requests ship is the proof point that makes the partnership feel real. Aim for at least one partner-suggested change to ship per quarter.

### Permissions

The partner grants Mimir Labs permission to:

- Use anonymized operational stories ("a 3-person CNC shop in Pennsylvania") in essays, marketing, sales conversations, and case studies, without further consent.
- Use attributed stories (named partner, named owner, photos of the shop) only with separate, explicit written consent per use. The default is anonymized.
- Reference the partnership existence in pitch decks, conversations, and content (e.g., "we have N design partners across manufacturing").

The partner retains full ownership of:

- Their operational data (job records, customer records, financials, etc.) entered into the Yggdrasil instance.
- Any IP, trade secrets, or competitively sensitive information they share during the dialogue.

Mimir Labs explicitly does not claim any rights over the partner's data, processes, customer relationships, or business practices. The partnership exchanges access for access, not ownership.

## Operating cadence

### Pre-execution (week 0)

- Identify the prospect against the qualifier table.
- Initial conversation (call or in-person) to confirm fit and explain the program.
- If both sides are interested, send the Design Partner Agreement (one-page MOU, Norn-signable — see template `AGREEMENT_TEMPLATE.md` once drafted).
- Sign and execute.

### Onboarding (week 1)

- Provision Yggdrasil instance.
- Schedule one-hour kickoff call.
- Send credentials, walkthrough doc, and calendar invites for the recurring monthly dialogue.

### Active partnership (months 1–24)

- Monthly dialogue call (recurring, on a fixed day-of-month if possible).
- Quarterly shop visit (in-person preferred).
- Feature requests / bug reports logged as they come up.
- 90-day review at month 3.

### Renewal or transition (month 24)

- Mutual conversation about whether to extend, transition to standard pricing, or end the relationship.
- If transitioning to commercial pricing, give 90 days notice and a generous ramp.

### Exit (any time after month 3)

- 30-day written notice from either party.
- Partner's data is exported and provided in a usable format.
- No claw-back, no penalty.

## Common pitfalls to avoid

1. **Don't onboard during a busy week.** The partner-machinist will resent the platform if it eats time during a week when their shop is slammed. Schedule onboarding for a week the partner says is light.
2. **Don't give them the whole platform.** Yggdrasil ERP is one thing. Offering Ratatosk/Bifrost/Ragnarok/Norn confuses what the relationship is and waters down the focus of feedback.
3. **Don't let the dialogue lapse.** Two missed months in a row is breach. Treat it that way. If the partner is too busy to engage, the partnership isn't working — better to acknowledge and unwind it than to pretend.
4. **Don't custom-build for them.** Their requests inform the roadmap. They do not become specific commitments. If the partner needs a custom feature, they need a different commercial relationship — handle separately or decline.
5. **Don't pitch up-market during the partnership.** The partner is not a stepping stone to selling Bifrost or Ratatosk. They're a feedback source. Pitching them to expand is the fastest way to lose them.
6. **Don't share their data with other partners.** Even casually. Cross-pollination of insights is fine in anonymized form; sharing one partner's specifics with another partner is a betrayal of the relationship.
7. **Don't romanticize the program.** It's a feedback acquisition mechanism. The partners are doing real work that has real cost on their side. Frame it that way, internally and externally.
