Enterprise Data & AI Capability Benchmark — Vendor Radar

Twenty-four functional conditions spanning enterprise data, ERP operations, AI/agents, and execution governance, scored 0–20 against a common rubric for SAP, Oracle, Microsoft, Salesforce, and Mimir Labs. The assessment measures whether a vendor's native architecture satisfies each function — not revenue, implementation workforce, or installed base. Incumbents are evaluated at full-portfolio level (e.g., Microsoft includes Purview, Fabric, Entra, and Copilot Studio, not Dynamics 365 alone).

Aggregate averages conceal the central finding. The incumbents achieve broad, relatively even profiles; Mimir Labs has a sharper one — exceptional scores concentrated in the execution-governance cluster (pre-write policy enforcement, state-transition validation, immutable auditability, deterministic execution), with its largest gaps in integration breadth and agent-platform tooling. The radar shape is more informative than the average.

Scoring rubric

Native capability outranks partner or custom-project capability; mandatory enforcement outranks optional configuration or after-the-fact remediation; architectural design is credited, but undeployed capability is discounted for operational maturity.

4–7 · LimitedNarrow, immature, or highly dependent on external products.
8–11 · PartialUseful capability, but material gaps in coverage, enforcement, or maturity.

Score rationale

Key conclusions. (1) The incumbents are not governance-naive — Oracle, SAP, Microsoft, and Salesforce all provide serious authorization, policy, audit, and agent controls; positioning that claims otherwise invites immediate rebuttal. (2) Incumbent governance remains heterogeneous — distributed across application logic, identity systems, workflow engines, catalogs, database constraints, and AI trust layers — and does not necessarily create one canonical admissibility boundary across a mixed estate. (3) Mimir Labs' strongest comparative claim is architectural concentration: discovering enterprise semantics, formalizing them canonically, governing boundary crossings, and preventing invalid operational states before commit. (4) Mimir Labs' largest weaknesses are execution risk, not conceptual gaps — connector breadth, deployed scale, tooling, localization, and reference customers trail the incumbents.

Limitations. Scores rely on public documentation and supplied Mimir Labs architecture materials; some Mimir Labs technical claims are not yet independently tested in a production customer environment. All 24 conditions are weighted equally. The benchmark deliberately does not score total cost, pricing transparency, vendor viability, implementation capacity, localization, or installed base — commercial dimensions that would materially favor incumbents on support and scale, and Mimir Labs on published formula-based pricing and per-tenant database isolation. A future version should replace analyst judgment with test protocols (unauthorized state transitions, split transactions, stale-state writes, schema drift) to convert this from a position paper into a measured benchmark.
