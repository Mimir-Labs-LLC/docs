Enterprise Execution Readiness Index 2026 — Vendor Radar

EERI evaluates whether enterprise platforms can support increasingly autonomous operations without allowing probabilistic actors, alternate tools, or integration paths to create invalid authoritative state. Eleven platforms — SAP, Oracle, Microsoft, Salesforce, Infor, Epicor, Sage X3, Acumatica, Odoo, Rappit, and Mimir Labs — scored 0–20 across thirty conditions in five domains, including six architecture-sensitive indexes: policy portability, governance-preserving extensibility, operational resilience, agent observability, concurrency and stale-state protection, and the authoritative execution surface itself.

Scoring rubric

Every score combines native capability, enforcement strength, breadth and consistency, and maturity evidence. Native outranks partner-only; mandatory enforcement outranks optional configuration; prevention outranks detection for execution-integrity conditions; architectural intent is credited only when testable and is discounted without deployment evidence. Evidence confidence is tracked separately from score (Rappit: medium overall, low-to-medium on runtime governance; Mimir Labs: high on architecture, low on commercial-scale maturity).

0–3 · AbsentNo meaningful native capability; substantial custom development required.
4–7 · LimitedNarrow, immature, or highly dependent on external products.
8–11 · PartialUseful capability with material gaps in coverage, enforcement, or maturity.

Key findings. Authoritative execution is a separate competitive axis — operational depth does not imply that semantic authority, current state, admissibility, concurrency control, and final commit are unified at one mandatory boundary. The major vendors already possess serious AI governance; the emerging gap is whether every authoritative mutation is technically forced through one state-aware admissibility surface. Governance-preserving extensibility is a distinct requirement. Agent observability is necessary but not sufficient. Policy portability determines whether governance is an enterprise asset or a product-specific configuration.

Buyer thresholds. For high-consequence autonomous writes, treat the six execution-integrity conditions (authoritative execution surface, pre-commit enforcement, state-transition validation, concurrency/stale-state protection, immutable audit, deterministic execution) as threshold requirements, not additive preferences — a high aggregate score should not compensate for failure at the required boundary. EERI's buyer-weighting table (§7.1) reweights the five domains by buyer profile.

Limitations. Portfolio evaluation favors large vendors on breadth. Mimir Labs receives direct architectural review unavailable to other vendors; to offset the asymmetry its commercial maturity, connector breadth, ecosystem, and production evidence are explicitly discounted, and its 20s remain subject to production validation. Equal weighting is an analytical convenience. Price, implementation capacity, installed base, localization, and vendor financial strength are not evaluated. The next EERI revision should be empirical: adversarial test protocols scored on observed behavior, not documentation.
