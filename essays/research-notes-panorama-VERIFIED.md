# Panorama Consulting Annual ERP Report — Verified Chart Data

**Verification date:** 2026-05-01
**Source PDFs:** Panorama Consulting Group, "The 20XX ERP Report" series
**Verification method:** Each PDF rendered page-by-page as image via the Read tool's multimodal PDF mode; numeric values transcribed from the rendered chart graphics, not from text extraction.

All four reports (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026) were obtained directly from Panorama's HubSpot CDN. Three have predictable URLs (`2023-ERP-Report-Panorama-Consulting.pdf`, `2024-erp-report-panorama-consulting-group.pdf`, `2026-erp-report-panorama-consulting-group.pdf`); the 2025 report uses a slightly different convention (`2025-ERP-Report-Panorama-Consulting.pdf`, capitalized, no "-group" suffix). The lowercase-with-group form for 2025 returns 404.

The "$200.5 million" median revenue and "150.5 median licenses" figures appearing in 2024, 2025, and 2026 reports look suspiciously like a recycled placeholder rather than re-measured year-over-year data. Worth flagging in the position paper.

**Note:** Median annual revenue dropped by ~85% between the 2023 and 2024 reports ($1.5 B → $200.5 M) and median employees dropped by ~90% (6,500 → 750.5). This is a sample-composition shift, not market change — the 2023 report explicitly described its respondents as "enterprise-sized" while 2024+ skews mid-market. Any year-over-year comparison spanning the 2023→2024 boundary is comparing different populations.

## Discrepancies between earlier-pass claims and verified-by-eye chart values

1. **"83% ROI realization rate" — partially correct but misleadingly cited.**
   The 83% figure exists *only in the 2023 report* (p. 27), only as a narrative sentence, and only for "respondents that performed an ROI analysis prior to their project AND have been live for at least a year" — a self-selected, doubly-filtered subset. It is not a population-wide ROI rate, and it does not appear in 2024, 2025, or 2026. Citing it as a current statistic is wrong; citing it as a Panorama-survey baseline without the filter caveat is misleading.

2. **"54% operational disruption rate post-go-live" — fabrication or misattribution.**
   No "operational disruption" metric exists in any of the four reports. The 54.3% figure in the 2024 report (p. 17) is the share of *AI-implementing respondents who sought BPM guidance*. The earlier pass conflated these. The 2025, 2026, and 2023 reports likewise contain no operational-disruption percentage. If the position paper needs an operational-disruption stat from Panorama, there isn't one — it would need to come from a different source (e.g., the older Panorama "ERP Report Card" or third-party studies like ASUG/Gartner).

3. **"65% cloud in 2023" — confirmed almost-correct (actual 64.5%).**
   The 2024 report's narrative statement that "only 65% of organizations selected cloud software" last year matches the 2023 report's 64.5% chart value, rounded up. Earlier-pass framing is fine.

4. **Customization distribution percentages — fabricated for 2024.**
   The 2024 report contains no customization breakdown chart at all. Any "X% no customization, Y% moderate, Z% heavy" attributed to the 2024 report is invented. The only year with a verifiable breakdown is 2023 (p. 18): 7.1% / 11.5% / 15.8% / 44.8% / 20.8% across five customization tiers.

5. **"More than half" on-time go-live — confirmed and quantified.**
   2023: 49.7% on time + 8.2% early = 57.9% within-or-under timeline.
   2024: 58.0% on time + 10.7% early = 68.7%.
   2025: 63.4% on time + 17.4% early = 80.8%.
   2026: 58.8% on time + 18.8% early = 77.6%.
   Panorama's "more than half" framing is conservative for all four years and dramatically understates 2025 and 2026.

6. **"More than half" on-budget — confirmed and quantified.**
   2023: 46.4% on budget + 6.6% under = 53.0% within-or-under.
   2024: 55.0% + 12.2% = 67.2%.
   2025: 53.5% + 15.1% = 68.6%.
   2026: 50.6% + 19.4% = 70.0%.

7. **Sample size N — verified.**
   2023: N=183. 2024: N=131. 2025: N=172. 2026: N=170. The drop from 183 to 131 between 2023 and 2024 (-28%) coincided with the dramatic shift in respondent profile (median revenue $1.5B → $200.5M). For an essay arguing about ERP failure modes, the 2024 report's 131-respondent sample is the smallest of the series and skewed mid-market.

8. **Top vendors selected — only available for 2023, not later years.**
   Microsoft (~32%), SAP (~23%), Oracle (~12%), Infor (~8%) per 2023 p. 9. Panorama dropped this question from 2024+; any "Panorama-says NetSuite is the top vendor" claim attributed to 2024+ reports is unsupportable from these PDFs.

9. **Time-to-go-live distribution — never charted; only a median.**
   No year provides a histogram of project durations. Each year states a single median (15.5 months in 2023 and 2024; 9 months in 2025 and 2026). The 2025/2026 figure is not directly comparable to 2023/2024 because the respondent profile changed significantly (more SaaS-heavy, more mid-market).

10. **Top reasons for cost overrun — verified, but the leading reason changed year-over-year.**
    2023: Project staffing underestimated (38.4%) was #1.
    2024–2026: "Additional technology needed" took over as #1 (51.2% → 51.9% → 54.9%).
    Any essay claim should specify which year and not assume a stable ranking.

11. **Top reasons for schedule overrun — verified, leading reason rotates each year.**
    2023: Technical issues (42.9%).
    2024: Resource constraints (56.1%).
    2025: Data issues (63.6%).
    2026: Organizational issues (57.9%).
    There is no stable "top reason" — the methodology and sample shift each year. This is itself worth noting as a critique of the Panorama series for citation purposes.

12. **Suspicious recurring numbers across years.**
    "$200.5 million" median revenue appears in both 2024 and 2026 reports. "150.5 median licenses purchased" appears in 2024 and 2025. "750.5 median employees" appears in 2024 and 2025. The probability of three independently-sampled cohorts producing the exact same medians (down to the half-unit) is essentially zero. Either Panorama is using a fixed segmentation cap on its respondent pool, or the figures are stale/copy-pasted from prior reports. Either way, these "respondent overview" numbers should be treated as nominal rather than precise.
